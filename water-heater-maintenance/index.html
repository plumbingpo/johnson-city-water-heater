Here is what shortens most water heaters' lives: corrosion from the inside and mineral buildup on the bottom, both of which are largely preventable. As water heats, dissolved minerals drop out of solution and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer insulates the water from the burner, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it buries the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, dissolves a little every month. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Johnson City, TN, we see this pattern every week, and most of it was preventable.

A Johnson City Water Heater visit is designed to stop both of those processes early. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, pull and inspect the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we check element resistance and thermostat operation and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, verify the temperature setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

Tankless heaters skip the sediment bed but have a scale problem of their own. Minerals coat the heat exchanger over time, reducing efficiency and eventually causing shutdowns. Our technicians connect a circulation pump to the isolation valves and run a descaling solution through the exchanger, clean the inlet screen, inspect the venting, and check the condensate drain on condensing models. When to Call for Water Heater Maintenance

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • Hot water with a sulfur odor
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • No memory of the last time the water heater was serviced
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
  • Keep the thermostat near 120 degrees to limit scalding and slow sediment buildup
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Consider a water softener if hard water keeps building sediment and scale
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
